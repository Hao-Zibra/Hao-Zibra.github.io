3 · Void growth under stack pressure — is impedance the early sensor?

A fixture holds a cell at 5 MPa, but the load is carried only by the parts of the interface still in contact. As voids open, the surviving annulus must carry the same force over less area, so the local pressure climbs as P/(1 − φ). The question this study answers is a reliability question, not a curiosity: by the time single-frequency impedance raises the alarm, is the ceramic already at risk?

STUDY RESULT Contact-pressure amplification and rim stress versus void coverage. (a) Exact statics requires the mean contact pressure to rise as P/(1 − φ), reaching 19.5 MPa at φ = 0.74 for a 5 MPa nominal stack load, shown against the ~120 MPa LLZO strength band and the ~1 MPa Li yield scale. (b) Finite-element maximum principal and von Mises stresses in the LLZO versus void coverage. Key result: the electrochemical alarm threshold (φ ≈ 0.2–0.35) is reached while rim stresses are still ~10–12 MPa, about five times below the LLZO strength band — single-frequency impedance is unambiguously the earlier sensor.

What this model does not claim. It is linear elastic throughout — real lithium yields near 1 MPa and creeps under pressure, so the lithium and rim stresses here are an elastic upper bound: the model quantifies the elastic driving force, not a healed steady state. The rim values are mesh-bounded estimates on a singular re-entrant edge, and one exported column (mean contact pressure) is invalid — its selection captured the full interface plane rather than the contact annulus, so the exact-statics column supersedes it. Both caveats are printed on the figure itself rather than left to a footnote.

This closes the loop with the fixture study on the CAD · FEA page: there, a compliant interlayer was needed to stop the platen riding the pellet rim; here, the same 5 MPa load meets a degrading interface, and the mechanics confirm that impedance sees the damage long before the ceramic is endangered.

Conclusion — why it matters

So what? Publishing the actual model files — solution-stripped, re-solvable, with an honest slimming ledger — is the simulation equivalent of publishing code: anyone with COMSOL can open, inspect, and re-run every claim this site makes about voids, thermal gradients, or fixture stress. The library also shows range: electrochemical impedance physics, coupled heat transfer, and contact mechanics, each validated against a published anchor or a second engine.
